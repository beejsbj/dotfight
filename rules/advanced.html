Book 2 is for players who like long games. Everything in the core rules still holds; the long war adds a page that fights back, and bases with shapes.

Read book 1, the core rules, first. Nothing here is played yet, and most of it is still to test.

Long war

  • Shaped bases: camps, prisms and cushions, each with its own walls (see 5).
  • Full pen physics: grooves, jolts, wall friction and ink cover (see 3 and 4).
  • Longer roads: sends walk for several turns (see 2).
  • Bigger armies than a Classic battle. to test

Sends

In a quick battle a convoy is on the road for one enemy turn. In the long war it walks for several turns, and you watch it walk in real time during each turn; it's exposed the whole way. to test lab: 150 units a turn, so a road takes two to four turns Still up to 5 at a time, and still once a turn.

Ink on the page

A page that's been fought over isn't blank any more, and the pen feels it.

  • Cross an old line and your hand jolts, slightly.
  • The angle decides. Meet a line at a shallow angle and the pen drops into its groove and is carried along it, like gravity. Meet it steeply and it jolts.
  • Your own grooves carry you further. Enemy grooves cut you short. to test

Ink as cover

Every line in a war runs at somebody's base, so ink piles up round the bases and becomes natural cover. That's on purpose: bases get safer as the page fills. strengths to test

Shaped bases

Each shape's walls have their own friction, and each shape does one thing to lines that come near it.

Circle, the camp

A gravity well. Lines passing near it, anyone's, yours too, bend round it, so you can curve a shot round a camp or through a gap. Its walls are soft, and it holds the most soldiers lab: 12, against 8 and 6.

Its pull is its garrison. The more soldiers inside, the harder it bends: a full camp bends lines hard, and as it's shot down the well weakens. Even an empty ring keeps a faint pull, the dent of the pen in the paper, and a send arriving strengthens it again. to test

Triangle, the prism

Your lines passing out through it split in two, so place it in front of your other bases. A lunger doesn't split: he's one body.

Hexagon, the cushion

Billiards. Its walls bank everyone's lines, yours too, by angle. Only a glancing line bounces lab: more than 34° off square; a straight one goes in.

Square and pentagon

Square, the eraser. A loose block on the page: lines that hit its walls shove it along, and as it slides it rubs out the ink beneath it, grooves and cover included. Shove it to clear a path, or into the ink round their camp. idea

Shaped soldiers

Soldiers are drawn in their base's shape: dots in a camp, little triangles in a prism, little hexagons in a cushion. For now it's only a look.

Their shape's power might wake up only in a last stand. idea

Tactics

The long war's page is a table to play off. What the shapes and the ink make possible:

  • Curve it round a camp. A full camp bends every line near it, so a shot can swing round it into a base you can't see straight. Your own camps bend your lines too.
  • Shoot the well down. A camp's pull is its garrison. Thin it and it stops bending, and your straight shots come back.
  • Bank off a cushion. A glancing line bounces off a hexagon, so you can reach behind a wall; come in square and you go through instead.
  • Put the prism in front. Your lines passing out through a triangle split in two. One flick through it covers two targets.
  • Ride your own grooves. Meet your old line shallow and it carries you on; meet theirs steep and take the jolt rather than be cut short.
  • Let the page fill. Ink piles up round bases and becomes cover. The open game is early; late on, you're shooting through scribble.
  • Walk the long road behind something. A convoy is out for several turns. Route it behind a cushion or a wall of ink.

Still to test

  • Jolts and grooves: how hard they shake and pull. Friendly ink carrying you and enemy ink slowing you was the least fair thing the lab tried (62% to whoever shot first).
  • Ink cover round bases. Intended, but the lab's version stalled games.
  • The circle as a well: how hard a full camp bends a line, how far out, and how fast it weakens as the camp is shot down.
  • Long-war sends: the pace, and how long a road should be.
  • Shape sizes (12, 6, 8) and the glance angle.
  • Bigger armies: how big.
  • Length: the lab's pen-physics war ran about 81 turns. For people who like long games, is that long enough, or too long?
