Page No. 1Date 26 · 9 · 26

Dawood made this game up at school. He and Burooj played it in grades 5 and 6, with ballpoint pens, on the back pages of their exercise books. Nobody wrote the rules down. Twenty years on, here they are: remembered, argued over, and written out properly.

The game on this site still plays the first, simpler version (shoot or move). These rules are next.

The page

Two players, a blue pen and a red one, and one sheet of paper. Everything drawn stays drawn. The dead are crossed out, never rubbed out, so a finished war is a page full of lines and crosses.

Setup

  1. Take turns drawing bases, one at a time, and jot each one full of soldier dots.
  2. Positioning. Before anyone flicks, each side arranges its soldiers: anywhere inside its bases, or a little outside the walls lab: 20 units, a dot and a bit. The first player arranges first, and the second gets to see it.

Nobody has to stay in, but it pays. Walls protect: a snipe loses power going through one and a lunger's hand shakes. And a lunger who lands inside a base dies.

A turn

one flick (a snipe or a lunge)+ one free send

Flicking

Pull back and let go, like flicking a pen stood on its tip. The harder the flick, the longer the line, and the wilder. A soft flick is short and sure.

Snipe

Your soldier stays put and the ink does the work. The line crosses out every enemy soldier it crosses. It goes straight through them.

Power loss. The paper fights back. Going through a base wall costs the line a lot of its power; going through a soldier costs less. The line thins and falls short. to test lab: 5% a wall and 10% a man were fine; 15% and 25% stalled games

Two with one bullet. Cross out two or more with one line and you flick again. It can chain, and power loss is what stops it: a line that pays for every wall and every man can't take many. to test

Lunge formerly “move”

Your soldier runs along his own ink, as far as a shot can go, and crosses out every enemy he runs through. He stands where the ink stops.

  • Walls shake him. Crossing a wall shakes his hand more than crossing a soldier does. It's the snipe's power loss, paid in shake instead. to test
  • Lunge again. Each kill earns another lunge by the same soldier, a little shakier every time lab: +0.05 rad, about 3°, a link. You can always stop.
  • He dies if he lands inside an enemy base, or runs off the page. Running through a wall is allowed, just shaky. Whoever he crossed out on the way stays crossed out.

Page No. 3Date 26 · 9 · 26

Send

Once a turn, and free: send soldiers walking from one of your bases to another. They walk in the open, slowly lab: 150 units a turn, so a road takes two to four turns, and any line that crosses them on the road crosses them out. Slowness is the price.

Bases

An emptied base doesn't vanish. It stays on the page as an empty ring, and a send can man it again.

Walls have friction: snipes lose power through them and lungers shake.

Ink on the page Long war

A page that's been fought over isn't blank any more, and the pen feels it.

  • Cross an old line and your hand jolts, slightly.
  • The angle decides. Meet a line at a shallow angle and the pen drops into its groove and is carried along it, like gravity. Meet it steeply and it jolts.
  • Your own grooves carry you further. Enemy grooves cut you short.
  • Ink piles up round the bases as the war goes on and becomes cover. That's on purpose: bases get safer with time. strengths to test

Last stand

When a side is down to its last 4, they've lost their comrades, and they fight like it: that side flicks twice a turn, with a steadier hand.

Winning

Cross out every enemy soldier.

Shaped bases Long war

Circle, the camp
Soft walls. Holds the most soldiers lab: 12, against 8 and 6.
Triangle, the prism
Your lines passing out through it split in two, so place it in front of your other bases.
Hexagon, the cushion
Banks everyone's lines, yours too, by angle, like billiards. Only a glancing line bounces lab: more than 34° off square; a straight one goes in.
Square and pentagon
being designed

Soldiers are drawn in their base's shape idea, and their shape powers might wake up only in a last stand idea.

Modes

Quick battle

Dawood's game. Circles only, with the core rules. You choose the size:

  • Quick 3 bases of 8 to test
  • Classic Dawood's 5 bases of 10
  • Custom pick the bases and the soldiers in each

Long war

Shaped bases (billiards), full pen physics (grooves, jolts, wall friction, ink cover) and bigger armies. For players who like long games.

Still to test

Every number on these pages is a guess from the rules lab, where a bot played itself a few thousand times. None of them has been felt at a real table yet.

  • Snipe power loss, a wall and a man. It's a core rule now, so its strength is the first thing to feel out.
  • Two with one bullet, with nothing else holding it back. The lab needed a rising bar to keep turns short (without it, up to 17 flicks in a turn). Does power loss do the job alone?
  • Lunge shake at walls and at men, and 3° a link.
  • How far outside the walls you may stand. Inside only changed nothing; further out turned the game into lunges.
  • Sends: the pace, and how many at once.
  • A lunger landing in an enemy's empty ring: does he die, walk in, or take it?
  • Grooves and jolts: how hard they pull and shake. Friendly ink carrying you and enemy ink slowing you was the least fair thing the lab tried (62% to whoever shot first).
  • Ink cover round bases. Intended, but the lab's version stalled games.
  • Quick at 3 bases of 8, and whether a war of about 50 turns is a phone game.
  • Shape sizes (12, 6, 8) and the glance angle.
